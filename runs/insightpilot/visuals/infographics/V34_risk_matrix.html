Why the fatal two stay high

A measurement is not a mitigation

R1's answer is to find out early and cheaply, which reduces exposure rather than risk. R2's answer is a set of dampeners against a field-wide capability limit. Neither engineers the problem away.

Three risks, one root

R1, R5 and R6 resolve together

If verification is genuinely cheap, R5's mitigation works and R6's cost is tolerable. If it is not, three rows fail at once. That is why one six-hour experiment is the declared stop-trigger.

Capstone versus venture

R8 is the dominant academic risk

For the venture, R1 and R2 are fatal. For 295A and 295B they are deliverables, since measuring either honestly is a good outcome whatever the answer. What threatens the capstone is running out of time to scope creep.

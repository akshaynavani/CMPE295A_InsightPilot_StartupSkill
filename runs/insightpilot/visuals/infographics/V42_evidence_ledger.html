Cautions that govern how the register may be used
one
The low-confidence rows are quarantined. Eight rows are vendor or aggregator content with no disclosed underlying study, and they appear only where the surrounding text names them as weak. One of them, a widely repeated claim that a data team spends 80% of its time on ad-hoc questions, supports nothing anywhere in this pack. It is registered so a later reader who meets the figure elsewhere knows it was found and rejected.
two
One heavily used row is a citation chain, not a primary source. The one-to-four-week turnaround figure reaches this pack through a large vendor restating three other firms' numbers. Those underlying reports were not reachable, so the figure is graded Medium and is always presented as reported-and-attributed rather than as a finding of the firm that published it.
three
Eight rows are registered and deliberately uncited. The market-size estimates were staged for a later phase because no early artifact had an argument that needed one, and the quality bar penalises a decorative number. Their spread is the reason to report a range: four estimates of the same market in the same year run from about $40B to about $50B, with growth rates between 8.67% and 14.98%.
four
One row is about five years old. Its fieldwork closed before the generative-AI cycle this pack depends on. It establishes that the overhead problem predates language models, which is genuinely useful, and nothing more. It must never be cited as a current measurement.
The discipline this enforces. A number without a source is a defect under the pack's own quality bar, so every figure either resolves to a row here or carries a visible flag saying it does not.
Five gaps: searched for, not found
1
No measured ad-hoc queue per analystEvery requests-per-week figure came from consultancy or vendor marketing with no stated method. Turnaround time is sourceable; queue length is not. This is the load-bearing gap in the problem statement.
2
No published accuracy benchmark from any commercial productFive major vendors describe mechanism and none publishes an execution-accuracy figure against any public benchmark. Every accuracy number here comes from a leaderboard or third-party analysis, never a vendor's claim.
3
No founder post-mortem from a failed natural-language BI companyThe graveyard is assembled from acquisition records and retirement notices, which establish that attempts ended and rarely say why in the operators' own words.
4
No disclosed pricing for three sales-qualified vendorsOne total-raised figure is the only hard number obtainable for any of them.
5
One likely graveyard entry could not be confirmedNo authoritative deprecation notice was found, so it is omitted rather than asserted.
Recording the absence matters as much as recording the finding. A later phase should not spend budget re-searching these blind.
What good grading bought

A rejected figure is worth registering

The most circulated number in this problem space was traced to unsourced vendor marketing and excluded. Writing down why is what stops it reappearing three artifacts later as common knowledge.

The gap that costs the most

Nobody publishes accuracy, so nobody can be compared

That absence is why this pack forbids head-to-head accuracy claims and why publishing one measured number would be its only genuine public contribution.

How to read the ledger as a skeptic

Start with the low rows and check where they were used

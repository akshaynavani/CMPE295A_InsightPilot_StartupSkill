InsightPilot · Minimum viable products

The first one is a document, and it can invalidate the second one entirely

Collapsing these two into a single MVP is the most expensive error available here. One tests whether the problem is real and costs about six founder-hours. The other tests whether the solution reaches usable accuracy and costs a semester. Keeping them distinct is what stops the semester starting before the afternoon.

First · weeks one to four

Low fidelity: the paper investigation

No software. Buildable in a day. About six founder-hours
What it is

One realistic multi-source question worked through by hand and formatted exactly as the system's export would be: the question, the plan as six numbered steps, each hop with its actual SQL and row counts in and out, the verification results including one amber coverage warning, the established facts, the doubt block, and a sign-off line.

Produced in variants: one correct, and three carrying planted defects. A wrong join grain, a wrong region mapping, a wrong time window.

The question it answers
Can an analyst reach a confident accept or reject on an answer they did not derive, faster than they could derive it, and do they catch it when it is wrong?
What it deliberately omits

Everything. No orchestrator, no agents, no connectors, no interface, no model calls. The artifact is a document and the entire test is what a person does with it.

That omission is the design. If verification is cheaper than re-derivation, it is cheaper because of the artifact's structure and not the software that produced it. Building the software first tests the same claim twelve weeks later at a hundred times the cost.

What falsifies it
Median cost ratio above 0.8. The differentiator is void. Stop and publish the negative result.
Defect rejection under 50%, or no lift over an answer-plus-SQL control. Worse than the first. The trace is comfortable rather than effective and would ship confident errors wearing an audit trail.

Second · weeks five to sixteen

High fidelity: the narrow path

One question type, two source types, end to end, measured
What is in
Evaluation harness and a fixed question set of thirty to fifty
Seeded-error set, the only instrument for silent-error rate
Postgres connector, read only
CSV and Excel through DuckDB, the unmodelled source no incumbent attempts
Plan generation, shown and approved before execution
Fetch and analytics agents, enough for a real question
Ambiguity interception and the binding store
Per-hop verification, the six structural checks
Hop lineage, single-hop re-run, exportable investigation
Doubt surfacing and the audit log
What is deliberately out, and why
Visualization agent. A table is an acceptable answer. Charts are the most demo-visible and least evidence-bearing thing available
REST connector. Two source types already demonstrate heterogeneity; a third proves nothing new
ML agent. Largest single build in the backlog, and already established as not the differentiator
Override re-flow. Degrades to invalidate and replan, which is the honest fallback anyway
Semantic-layer consumption. Next semester. Important for the buyer, not for the core premise
Any polish. The output is a measurement, not a demo
The question it answers
Does multi-hop planning across heterogeneous sources reach usable accuracy on a narrow path, and do cheap structural checks catch the failures that matter?
What falsifies it
Execution accuracy under 30%. The mitigations do not overcome the field's baseline. Narrow to two-hop questions and publish the low number anyway.
Near zero catch rate on the semantically plausible defect class. Say that the verifier catches mechanical and not semantic failures, and make the shown plan the primary defence.
Earlyvangelist: five things already done
1
Wrote SQL against production last weekTo answer someone else's ad-hoc question. This establishes the queue.
2
Hit a wall with an existing copilotTried one, abandoned it, and can name the specific reason. They are not comparing against nothing.
3
Worked around a missing sourceReconciled a file or an export against a warehouse by hand, and can say how long it took.
4
Been burned by an unverified numberMost likely to be fudged. "I worry about accuracy" is not it. The question is about the last time they sent an answer that turned out wrong, and with no story the pain is theoretical.
5
Installed an open-source data tool themselvesDisqualifies the most people and is not negotiable. At this contract value, a user who needs procurement cannot be reached by any channel this venture can afford.
Past behaviour, not stated interest. Someone meeting fewer than all five is a friendly conversation rather than a design partner, and the failures are data in their own right. If nobody meets the third, the multi-source thesis is already in trouble.
What neither MVP tests
Whether anyone pays. Out of scope. The budget question is a conversation, not an MVP
Whether the flywheel compounds. Needs ninety days of real use, which no MVP reaches
Whether the review gate scales. Needs several stakeholders submitting to one analyst over weeks, and the high-fidelity version has one user
Whether the security posture survives a real review. Built for it, untested against it
Why the order is not negotiable

The cheap test can invalidate the expensive one, and not the reverse

If verification costs as much as re-derivation, a more accurate planner does not help, because the analyst still cannot use an answer they did not derive.

Why this and not a landing page

A sign-up measures the pitch, which is not the risk

The risk is behavioural and specific: does reading a trace cost less than doing the work. Only a timed task with a real analyst and a real artifact answers that, and a document is sufficient for it.

What viable means here

A number that is true, publishable and re-runnable

Not usable by a customer. Since no commercial product in this category publishes an accuracy figure, a working measured narrow system is a stronger artifact than a broad unmeasured demo.

Verify is a beat in the loop, not a footnote after it

Every incumbent assistant ends at Answer and hands the analyst the SQL. That makes verification the analyst's unpaid work, which is the state they are already in. Naming Verify as a beat gives it its own surface, its own latency budget and its own metric.

Why the beat exists

Failure looks exactly like success

Wrong answers are plausible, not obviously broken. Schema-level errors, meaning a wrong column or a misread definition, account for the overwhelming majority of analysed text-to-SQL failures.

81.2%  of 4,602 faulty queries
What it measures

Time-to-verified-answer

Wall-clock from question asked to an answer the analyst will stake their name on. It contains both speed and trust, so it cannot be gamed by answering fast and wrong.

< 0.5  target verification cost ratio
The untested premise

Checking must beat rebuilding

The beachhead's own objection: “I read SQL faster than I read explanations.” If checking costs what deriving costs, this beat is ceremony. Unmeasured anywhere in the literature.

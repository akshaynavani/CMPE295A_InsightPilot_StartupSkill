InsightPilot · Survey

The case against is stronger on reliability and weaker on whether the position is worth occupying

A survey that only argues for its sponsor's mechanism is advocacy, so the opposing case is stated at full strength and runs two items longer than the supporting one. Both readings survive the evidence, and the resolution turns out to be architectural rather than rhetorical.

For the mechanism 6
1
The strongest incumbent independently confirms decompose-then-verify. Its self-check decomposes a query into smaller verification statements and regenerates on failure. Arrived at from the opposite direction, which makes it real corroboration
2
The connector layer is now a configuration problem. The standard is dated, multi-vendor and neutrally stewarded, which is exactly the stated condition for a pluggable layer being tractable for a small team
3
Demand for verifiability is measured, not assumed. 71% of practitioners name wrong output reaching stakeholders as a top concern, 24.9% name accuracy as their top reservation, and trust in data rose from 66% to 83% year over year
4
The multi-hop cross-source position is genuinely unoccupied. Every warehouse-native assistant is single-warehouse and effectively single-hop, and the semantic-layer market is an integration surface rather than a rival
5
The predictive step is absent from every shipped product. The capability exists in the literature and no BI product exposes it
6
The problem predates the hype. 44% of data engineers' time on building and rebuilding pipelines, measured in 2021, before this cycle. The overhead is not an artifact of current enthusiasm
Against, at full strength 8
1
The benchmark evidence contradicts the mechanism's central requirement. The orchestrator is exactly the capability measured at roughly 15% on hard real tasks. Not early. Measured, and low. A reviewer finds this in one search.
2
Compounding is arithmetic, not engineering debt. Four to six hops at 95% each lands near 74%, and at 90% near 53%. That is arithmetic rather than a measurement of this system. Every hop added to make it more capable makes it less reliable
3
The incumbents' restriction is a considered answer, not a limitation. A table ceiling and a token-capped model read better as the field's settled response: constrain scope until reliability is acceptable. Remove the constraints and you inherit the unreliability they were bought to prevent
4
Gravity has taken every standalone attempt so far. One raised about $128.7M and became a warehouse division; another was folded into a BI vendor. Neither failed technically
5
The purest market test of the low edge was marked down 73.67%. Well funded, early, technically credible. The natural-language-for-non-analysts thesis has not paid off at the price it was funded at
6
The nearest neighbour is open source, two years ahead, and has a community. Thirteen thousand stars and ten thousand cloud users. The difference is scope of loop, which is a quantitative difference
7
A commodity trap sits at the entry point. The day-one spreadsheet path is already served at $35 a month
8
Forecasts cut both ways. The same analysts predicting 40% agent penetration by the end of 2026 predict over 40% of agentic projects cancelled by the end of 2027, and half of deployment failures traced to governance
The assessment

A system promising trustworthy autonomous answers would be promising what the field cannot currently deliver, and a pack claiming otherwise would be the graveyard's next entry.

But that is not the only product the same evidence supports. It equally supports a system whose design centre is making failure cheap to detect: verification per hop, replanning on invalidation, and a trace structured so the analyst checks the one hop they doubt instead of re-deriving the chain.

On that reading the low reliability numbers are the reason for the architecture rather than an objection to it, and the market's own top-ranked concern is the demand signal for exactly that.
Three consequences that bind downstream work
1
Lineage is not a feature. It is the answer to the first objection, and it has to be specified as load-bearing in the requirements. The bar is a notebook where every step is visible and re-runnable, not "we show the SQL."
2
The outcome metric is right and should not move. Time to a verified answer prices verification into the measurement. An accuracy-only metric would hide the failure mode that actually matters, which is plausible but wrong.
3
Measure multi-hop accuracy on one narrow path and publish it. No commercial product publishes one. Measuring it is both the honest response to the strongest objection and the most credible artifact the project could produce.
The split the survey found
Single-hop text-to-SQL on realistic schemas is strong, with top systems near 96.7%. Multi-step data reasoning is largely unsolved, at roughly 15% on hard tasks against 76% on easy ones, with consistency collapsing from above 60% on one attempt to below 25% across eight, and error propagation named the primary bottleneck.
What changed between generations. Language models removed the linguistic parsing bottleneck that defeated the previous attempts and replaced it with a semantic grounding bottleneck. The field converged on hand-authored semantic layers to solve it, which buys correctness at the price of scope.
The method's limits, stated by the survey itself

This is desk research and says so

Thirty-three searches and six direct page fetches on one day. No analyst was interviewed, no product was installed, and no measurement in it is the authors' own. Five gaps that were searched for and not found are recorded rather than passed over.

Why the survey exists at all

A claim without a citation is a defect, not a style choice

The project concedes its problem is researched rather than lived, which makes an unsourced technical claim the likeliest point of collapse under review.

The conclusion in one line

Well posed, currently unbuilt, and the risk is correctly named

The evidence supports building verification affordances rather than betting on end-to-end reliability.

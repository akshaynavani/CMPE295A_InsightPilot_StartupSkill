InsightPilot · Value proposition

The top three fits are one mechanism sold to three people for three reasons

Ranked by pain severity times relief strength, which is the only ranking that predicts adoption. That concentration is coherent and it is also the concentrated risk, because all three rest on one untested premise.

Where the fit is weak or absent
1
It does not relieve the low edge's pain. The requester cannot verify, so the central gain creator is inaccessible to him. His only path is an analyst's sign-off, which means the system must make sign-off cheap rather than make him self-sufficient. Anything else is the bet the market marked down
2
It does not relieve the data-quality pain underneath everything. 77% report data-quality issues and 91% say those issues affect performance. Perfect lineage over bad data produces a beautifully traceable wrong answer, and the product should say so rather than let a buyer assume otherwise
3
It does not reduce headcount, and pretending it might is the fastest way to lose a buyer who has heard it before
4
The fourth fit is where competition actually lives. A demo that leads with it is a demo of a worse version of the bundled incumbent
Why three personas and not five
The low edge shapes the design and does not buy; his questions arrive through the analyst, and a value proposition written for him is the bet that was marked down 73.67%. The platform lead has a veto, not a value proposition; his requirements are security constraints. The three that remain are the three whose value actually differs.
What the ranking says

Coherent, and a single point of failure

One mechanism relieves the top three pains for three different people. If the first fit's premise is false, all three collapse together, and the timed task that settles it takes an afternoon.

The operational rule

Lead with the first fit, never the fourth

The fourth is table stakes and invites the head-to-head comparison the pack forbids. The first is the only mapping that serves all three personas at once.

The failure this page exists to catch

A best feature relieving a pain nobody ranked highly

That is what happened to the natural-language feature retired from the most widely deployed BI tool on earth after a decade. Distribution did not save it. The curation-to-value ratio killed it.

InsightPilot · User spectrum

One adaptive system, three edges, and no separate tiers

The interface adapts to what a person can verify. Three things change across the edges: whether the plan renders expanded, whether ambiguity is phrased in business terms or column names, and whether export is gated on sign-off. There is no business edition to maintain.

Low edge
Marcus
Regional sales director

Consumes answers. Cannot judge correctness and knows it. Opens a dashboard twice a month and does not trust it.

Needs: a plain answer, and to know whether it is solid enough to put on a slide.
“How do I know it's right? I can't read a query.”
Beachhead
Priya
Senior data analyst

Writes SQL fluently. Absorbs a queue of ad hoc questions while trying to protect planned work.

Needs: to compress per-question overhead without giving up control or verifiability.
“I read SQL faster than I read explanations.”
High edge
Dr. Chen
Staff data scientist

Reviews other people's analyses. Distrusts automated modelling after a year spent cleaning up leakage.

Needs: to spot-check the one step he doubts instead of re-deriving the whole chain.
“Automated modelling is how you ship leakage.”
Why each edge earns its place

The edges make two features load-bearing

Without the low edge, natural language is decorative for a beachhead that already writes SQL. Without the high edge, traceability is cosmetic. Each edge is the reason one signature feature has to work, rather than a segment to sell to.

The risk this spectrum carries

It was derived, not observed

No analyst was interviewed. The three edges are an inference, and the PRD, the UX spec, all four journeys and the persona set are written against them together. If the spectrum is wrong, five artifacts are wrong at once.

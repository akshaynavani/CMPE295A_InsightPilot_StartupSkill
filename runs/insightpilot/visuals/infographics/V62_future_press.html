InsightPilot · Working backwards

Written in 2026, dated 2033, and the good ending is losing control of the differentiator

Working backwards forces a question the pack had deferred: is success a company or a practice? The discipline is that a release announcing a very large business would contradict the market sizing and the channel this pack already committed to, so it does not announce one.

The release, 14 March 2033
San Jose
The signed answer becomes the default unit of analytical work The signed investigation, an analytical answer shipped with a hop-level trace and a named human reviewer, has passed 50% adoption among large data organisations.
Seven years earlier, an ad-hoc question took one to four weeks and 76% of businesses admitted deciding without data. The industry's response was to put chat interfaces on warehouses, which answered quickly and were bounded to questions someone had modelled in advance. So the ad-hoc question stayed unanswered or unverifiable.

We stopped trying to convince people our answers were right. We built a system that assumes it will sometimes be wrong and makes that cheap to discover. They never wanted to be told to trust a black box. They wanted to check it in forty seconds instead of ninety minutes.

the founder

The worst outcome used to be that the analysis turned out fine, because I had spent two hours confirming something with no way to spend less next time. Now I open the trace, check the target definition and the split, and spot-check the one join I do not like. Nineteen minutes.

Before 2027, analytics organisations measured query latency and dashboard adoption, both of which can improve while trust falls. Time to a verified answer was not a tracked metric anywhere.

Five years ago vendors competed on how confident their AI sounded. Now they compete on how fast you can prove it wrong. That reframing is the company's real legacy, and most of the market has adopted it without adopting their software.

an industry analyst
How it happened
2026
A two-person capstone builds the harness before the agents, publishes the first public multi-hop accuracy figure in commercial agentic BI, and runs the timed verification study with eleven analysts. The study is the reason there is a company: the ratio comes back at 0.4, not above 1.0
2027
Open-source release. First deployment through an analyst who found the repository in a community thread. A second analyst in that company installs after receiving an exported investigation, and the compounding loop closes for the first time
2028
The review-queue problem nearly kills adoption. Analysts absorb the verification burden their colleagues shed and stop advocating. Fixed by trust levels per question shape, so repeat investigations auto-approveThe risk raised three phases before launch. It fired, and was survived by design change rather than by luck
2029
Override rate per source falls measurably across long-running deployments with no model change. The learned semantic layer is evidenced for the first time, three years after it was named as an unproven candidate and deliberately not claimed
2030
The lineage format is contributed to a neutral foundation and two warehouse vendors adopt it. The company loses exclusivity over its differentiator and gains the standard
2031
Managed deployment for regulated customers. Neutrality is preserved contractually, because the format is now a standard the company does not own
2033
Verification cost ratio enters procurement requirements. The practice outgrows the product
Four checks, because this is where a vision contradicts its own evidence
1
No hypergrowth and no venture-scale outcome. Seventy-four million across nineteen hundred organisations is consistent with the serviceable market sized in 2026. Announcing a multi-billion business would contradict the pack's own sizing, which states plainly that this market is small
2
No order-of-magnitude claim, in 2033 either. The original arithmetic came to roughly 2.9 times on multi-source questions and about 1.0 on familiar single-source ones. The outcome is a practice change, from a ratio near 1.0 down to 0.31, which is a different kind of claim and the one the mechanism actually supports
3
The moat is evidenced in 2029, not asserted in 2026. The confirming observation was named and declined at the start. The timeline observes it three years after launch, which is how long a compounding semantic asset would actually take
4
The company loses control of the differentiator and that is the good ending. With an open-source core the code was never the asset. A release where the company still exclusively owns hop-level lineage in 2033 would contradict its own licensing decision
And one thing the release cannot do. It assumes the 2026 verification study came back below 1.0. If that ratio had been above 1.0, none of this happens.
The honest press release for that branch is one paragraph long. It says the team measured the thing that mattered, found the premise false, and published the negative result. That would still have been a good capstone.

The riskiest cell is empty, and it is empty on purpose

The compression test: a venture that needs a page to explain its problem does not have one. If the problem, the solution and the advantage cannot each be stated in three bullets without contradicting each other, something upstream is wrong.

1Problem
Every ad-hoc question costs the same fixed manual sequence, and the requester waits one to four weeks at enterprise scale
Answers that arrive fast are not trusted. 71% name wrong output reaching stakeholders as a top concern, and verifying an answer you did not derive may cost what deriving it costs
The predictive question is usually not attempted at all, because it is a different job rather than a slower one
2Existing alternatives
Doing nothing. 76% have decided without consulting data because access was too hard. The market leader
The analyst writes the SQL. Correct, unverifiable by anyone else, does not scale past one person's attention
Warehouse-native assistants. Single source, single hop, bounded by a pre-authored artifact
3Solution
An orchestrator that decomposes a question into a plan spanning heterogeneous sources, and replans when a result invalidates it
Four specialist agents, each handoff emitting inspectable, re-runnable lineage
Open core and self-hostable, running inside the customer's boundary on their own model key
4Key metrics
Time to a verified answer. The outcome metric, and it contains both speed and trust
Verification cost. Minutes to accept or reject an answer, against minutes to re-derive it
Multi-hop execution accuracy on a fixed question set. The number no commercial product publishes
5Unique value proposition

Not faster. Checkable

The only conversational analytics layer that answers a question spanning your real sources and hands back an investigation you can verify hop by hop
Checkable is what makes an answer you did not derive worth having at all
For the enterprise analyst who can already write the query, not the business user who cannot
Second riskiest cell. It asserts that verification is cheaper than re-derivation, and the beachhead persona's own objection says the opposite. Testable in an afternoon, and untested
riskiest cell
6Unfair advantage

None yet, and none claimed

An open-source core means the code is not the asset
Strongest candidate is a learned semantic layer: accumulated disambiguation of one organisation's schema, metrics and vocabulary. Copyable code on non-copyable semantics
Confirming evidence would be acceptance rate rising over time with no model change. Not yet observed
7Channels
Open-source distribution: the repository, the documentation, the communities where the beachhead already reads
Analyst-led adoption, buyer-closed. The analyst advocates and the budget owner signs
The academic channel during the capstone, where a published accuracy number is the credible artifact
8Customer segments

The analyst, not the business user

Beachhead: enterprise data analysts at organisations with a deployed BI stack, an ad-hoc queue, and questions spanning more than one source. About 74,600 in the US, built bottom up from labour data plus stated assumptions
Economic buyer: the analytics function's budget owner
Early adopters: analysts who already tried a warehouse copilot and hit its bounded-scope wall
9Cost structure
Model inference per investigation across several agents, several hops and retries, anchored against a market price of about $0.134 per message
Connector engineering and maintenance. The standard made access a configuration problem and did nothing for semantics
Zero data-storage cost, because the customer runs it
10Revenue streams · hypothetical by decision
Monetisation is out of scope for the academic project, and this cell is empty by choice rather than omission
Modelled threshold of $75 per analyst per month, at the top of the observed analyst-tool band. A price to falsify, not a forecast
The incumbents already price the question, billing per message or capping an agent at 25 queries a month, which matches this product's unit of value
Why the empty cell is the risk

It fails for the opposite of the usual reason

Most canvases fail here by asserting a moat that does not exist. This one fails by having nothing to assert and saying so. With an open core and no evidenced compounding loop, a well-executed copy is a legitimate outcome, and the pack should own that rather than paper over it.

The instruction that comes with it

Do not fill block six in a later phase

Manufacturing a moat to make the canvas look complete would cost the pack exactly the credibility the empty cell earns it.

What this canvas drops

Three blocks live next door

Key partners, key activities and customer relationships are a deliberate simplification here. They sit in the nine-block canvas, where every block also carries a falsifiable hypothesis and the cheapest test that would kill it. Neither page is complete alone.

Most of the channels do not compound. One does, and the plan is a bet on it

A channel list where everything is equally weighted is a way of not deciding. This one starts from a concession rather than an advantage, runs a single channel first, and names the product requirement the loop secretly depends on.

The buyer already owns a competing product, bundled and paid forEvery conversation opens by explaining why the thing they have is not this thing
The product is open core and self-hostableWhich makes the primary channel a repository rather than a sales team, and the first conversion an install rather than a meeting
There is no revenue and no sales functionThe ninety-day motion below is what a small capstone team can actually run, not what a funded company would

The loop that compounds, and where it breaks
An analyst installs the open coreNo purchase order, no meeting, no vendor relationship
Runs an investigation on their own sourcesTheir question, their data, their judgment of the answer
The investigation and its lineage are shareableA dashboard is shareable and not checkable. A chat answer is neither
Where the loop breaks, honestlyA colleague sees a verifiable answer and asks where it came from. This edge only exists if the artifact is readable outside a running instance. If lineage lives only inside the app, there is no edge here and growth reverts to whatever the repository does alone
A second analyst installsAnd the loop closes
Enough internal use that a budget owner is asked to fund it
the loop closes back to step one
The engine is that an investigation is a shareable artifact. With its lineage it is shareable and checkable at once, which makes forwarding it a professional act rather than a risk. This is the differentiator used as distribution, which is the strongest form of a growth loop, because improving the product improves the loop.
A product requirement disguised as a go-to-market requirement. An investigation must export as a self-contained readable artifact. It is the highest-leverage decision on this page, nearly free to build early, and expensive to retrofit later.

Deliberately not in the ninety-day motion

No outbound, no paid acquisition, no partnerships, no sponsorship, no landing-page tuning

Each would spend the scarcest resource available, which is engineering hours, to buy something the loop is supposed to produce for free. Every figure in the acquisition-cost hypothesis is an assumption with no funnel behind it, and is labelled as one. The payback logic matters and the values do not.

The reframe that makes the plan work

Treat the benchmark as the go-to-market asset, not a research output

Scope the spike so the harness, the question set and the write-up are shippable. One artifact is simultaneously the academic deliverable, the credibility proof for the internal sceptic, and the top of the funnel.

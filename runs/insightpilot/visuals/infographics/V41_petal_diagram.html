InsightPilot · Petal diagram

The empty quadrant has no budget attached to it

A two-by-two asks where rivals sit. The petal asks which existing budget and which existing habit this displaces. The answer is uncomfortable and coherent: customers come from the petal with no budget line, the aligned budget sits in the hardest petal to win, and the largest budget belongs to a supplier.

Petal 1
primary source

Analyst time

The status quo. The analyst writing SQL by hand, no vendor involved
Salary · unbudgeted as tooling
Switches when an answer they did not derive becomes cheaper to accept than to write. Their current method never fails on correctness, only on latency, and that asymmetry is why it is durable. No competitor has to lose and no procurement is triggered.
Petal 2
additive, not a switch

Warehouse-native copilots

Genie · Cortex Analyst · Power BI Copilot · Pulse · Spotter
Bundled, plus metered AI credits
Switches when they hit the bounded-scope wall: a source outside the platform, or a metric that is not already a measure. Pre-qualified on the exact pain. But the bundled incumbent cannot be cancelled, so this is an addition to justify rather than a replacement.
Petal 3
supplier, not source

dbt Semantic Layer · Cube · AtScale · Honeydew
59% of large orgs adding budget
The largest active budget in the diagram, and the wrong one to draw from. It answers what a metric means, which this product does not answer and depends on.

Petal 5

Data science and automated ML

AutoML platforms and internal data-science headcount
Headcount, overwhelmingly
Switches when nothing does, in year one. Agent medal rates on real competitions run against a twelve-hour budget, which is real capability on a timescale that is not conversational. It is on the diagram because it is where expansion would come from, and naming it stops a later phase discovering it as if it were new.
Petal 4
budget and price align

Analyst notebooks

Hex · Deepnote · Jupyter
$36 to $75 per editor/month
Switches when they want a plan rather than a canvas. The honest competitive petal: these customers understand the proposition immediately and are hardest to win, because the notebook already solves auditability by making them do the work. The only petal where spend, buyer and price all line up.

4
Semantic layer. The largest active budgetWrong budget. It is a supplier petal
5
Data science. The expansion storyNot year one
Why the largest budget is the wrong one. Drawing from it means telling the buyer that the thing she is currently funding is unnecessary. It is not. The highest-leverage move available is to consume her existing semantic layer and inherit correctness work she already paid for, which turns the largest petal from a competitor into a supplier and defuses her hardest objection.
A venture that mistakes an adjacent budget for a displaceable one picks a fight with a decision the buyer has already made. Answering "I am buying a semantic layer for exactly this" with "you should not be" loses the deal.
Consequence one, structural

Adoption precedes revenue by a long way

The primary source petal has no budget line, so users arrive well before money does. That is fine for a capstone and has to be stated rather than glossed in any investor-facing framing.

Consequence two, and it is counterintuitive

Prepare for the notebook comparison, not the warehouse one

The bundled copilot is the question everyone asks. The notebook is the competitor whose customers have both the budget and the understanding, which makes it the more informative conversation.

Consequence three, the test

The buyer conversation is a budget-source conversation

Ask what line this comes out of and what it displaces, verbatim. A buyer who cannot name one has answered the market-type question more decisively than any survey could.

InsightPilot · Prior attempts

Four attempts ended. None of them died of bad technology

Conversational access to enterprise data has been attempted continuously since at least 2014. The pattern matters more than the list: two died of the curation-to-value ratio, and two were absorbed by whoever owned the data. Both failure modes are live for this project, and only one of them has an answer.

2019

IBM Watson Analytics

Natural-language questions over uploaded business data. The flagship of its generation.
Discontinued June 2019, folded into Cognos Exploration. The wider Watson line sold Watson Health for about $1B after roughly $5B invested.
Demoed well. Did not survive contact with real schemas and real users.
2021

Narrative Science

Natural-language generation over BI. Founded 2010, partnered with Tableau since 2016.
Acquired by Salesforce in December 2021 and folded into Tableau. Terms undisclosed.
Narration is a feature, not a company. Absorbed by the platform that owned the data.
2023

Sisu Data

Diagnostic decomposition. Automatically explaining why a metric moved.
Raised about $128.7M. Acquired by Snowflake in October 2023, standalone product in wind-down.
Attacked the highest-value part of the loop, well funded, and still ended as a warehouse feature.
2026

Power BI Q&A

Natural-language querying via a curated linguistic schema: synonyms, relationships, teach Q&A.
Fully retired by end of December 2026. Existing Q&A visuals stop working. Users directed to Copilot.
Shipped inside the most widely deployed BI tool on earth. Distribution was never its problem.
Failure mode one, and its answer

The curation-to-value ratio

Watson and Q&A both demanded that modellers pre-encode meaning: synonyms, linguistic relationships, explicit corrections. The burden exceeded the value returned. This project's answer is to consume a semantic layer where one exists and never require one, which is principle P6. Note that Microsoft replaced Q&A with an approach that inherits the same dependency on a well-prepared model.

Failure mode two, unanswered by evidence

Gravity, and why this is not a warehouse feature

Narrative Science and Sisu were absorbed by whoever owned the data. The only structural answer this research supports is cross-source neutrality: the value is in spanning, and a warehouse vendor cannot sell spanning without arguing against its own gravity. That is an argument, not an observation, and it stays untested until a real customer's questions are examined.

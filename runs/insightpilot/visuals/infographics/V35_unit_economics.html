The margin looks excellent because the customer absorbed the variable cost

Self-hosting moves inference onto the customer's own key, which inverts the usual analysis. The honest question is not what this venture earns per analyst but what the deployment costs the customer to run, and that figure rests on a number nobody in the category publishes.

Verification, in cost terms
All six structural checks are plain SQL against the customer's own warehouse. Zero model calls, about six cents of compute per investigation. The differentiating mechanism is the cheapest line in the system. A design built on model self-critique would have tied the differentiator to token price instead.

The cost curve, honestly read

Falling model prices are the customer's tailwind, not a margin story

A threefold decline takes the three-attempt case from $524 to roughly $175 per analyst-year. Our margin does not move, because it contains no inference. Cheaper inference also makes it cheaper for incumbents to widen their bounded scope.

The counter-pressure worth naming

Retries matter more than price declines

Over a two-year horizon, getting expected attempts from three down to one is worth more than a threefold fall in token price. Engineering beats the market curve here.

The metric that should lead instead

Deployments reaching a second analyst

At zero acquisition cost, expansion without founder intervention is what turns a free install into a paid account. That, and not margin, is the number that decides whether the economics exist.

A green tick means these checks passed. It never means the answer is right

This is the most consequential detail in the interface. A tick that reads as "correct" would turn the verify surface into false reassurance, which is worse than having no trace at all. The distinction has to survive a busy user glancing at it, which makes it a design obligation rather than a documentation one.

How it must render

Always with the named check and its numbers

✓ Step 3  join preserved row count (14,201 → 14,201)
not checked: semantic grain, time basis

The tick never stands alone. Hovering states what was not checked. The unchecked set is a stored field, not a runtime detail, so an omission stays auditable months later.

What it must never become

A bare mark that reads as a verdict

✓ Step 3  verified
 

Dr. Chen read the ticks correctly as "these structural checks passed." A less careful reviewer must be unable to read them wrongly, and hierarchy alone may not achieve that.

The residual, priced in

The whitepaper discounts itself for this

The reconciliation mechanism was observed at 6.7x in the worked example and is claimed at 4.0x. The difference is exactly this uncertainty: the checks provably catch countable failures, and their coverage of plausible ones is unmeasured. The seeded-error set exists to replace that judgement with a number.

The fallback, stated in advance

If hierarchy is not enough, remove green entirely

The spec concedes that treatment may not stop a busy user pattern-matching a tick to "correct." The named fallback is to show only amber and red, so the absence of a warning carries no positive claim. Worth testing rather than assuming.

InsightPilot · Business model canvas

Nine blocks, each carrying the cheapest test that would kill it

One canvas says what the business is. This one says what would have to be true and how little it costs to find out it is not. The column that matters is the last pair: a wrong assumption found over a week of conversations rather than halfway through a build.

Customer segmentsanalyst, budget owner, platform blocker
A material share of the ad-hoc questions an enterprise analyst receives require data from more than one source
Ten analysts classify their last twenty real requests as single or multi-source. Five conversations. Under about 25% and the neutrality argument is true and worthless, and the addressable market halves
this month

Value propositionsan investigation with hop-level lineage
An analyst can accept or reject a correct answer they did not derive, faster than they could derive it themselves
Timed task, five analysts, same question, two conditions. Minutes to a confident yes or no. This is the beachhead's own objection and the single most important test in the pack. One afternoon
one afternoon

Channelsrepository, communities, analyst-led adoption
Analysts will install a self-hosted open-source analytics tool without a vendor relationship
Publish the repository with a working quickstart and count installs reaching a first successful investigation over thirty days. Free, and it doubles as the growth loop's first data point
30 days
4
Customer relationshipsself-serve, community, ten design partners
A self-serve open-source motion is sufficient to reach first use with no sales conversation
Instrument the quickstart. Median clone to first successful investigation above thirty minutes and self-serve fails, the motion is really design-partner led, and both the go-to-market and the channel plan change materially
with first installs

Revenue streamshypothetical by decision
A budget owner would pay about $900 per analyst per year for verifiability, on top of the BI platform they already own
Not a pricing survey. A budget-source question to five buyers: what line does this come out of, and what does it displace. A buyer who cannot name a line has already answered
two weeks
6
Key resourcescapability, not assets
The learned semantic layer compounds: acceptance rate rises over time with no model change
Instrument acceptance rate per organisation from day one and check the slope after ninety days of real use. Cannot be tested before deployment, which is exactly why no moat is claimed and why this row must not be inflated later
90 days deployed
7
Key activitiesplanning, connectors, lineage, the harness
Per-hop verification measurably raises end-to-end accuracy over an unverified baseline
Build the fixed question set first, then measure with and without verification. This is the first-semester spike, tested against a hard-task baseline of roughly 15%
first semester
8
Key partnerssemantic layers, connector standard, model providers
Consuming an existing dbt or Cube semantic layer materially improves accuracy over raw schema introspection
The same question set against one schema, with and without its semantic layer attached. A large gain makes this the highest-leverage integration in the roadmap; a small one makes the semantic-layer story weaker than assumed
4 days
9
Cost structureinference, connectors, zero storage
Cost to serve one investigation stays below the threshold the modelled price implies, at realistic question volume
Instrument token spend per investigation in the spike and multiply by an assumed monthly volume. Computable from the architecture, which is why unit economics is retained while revenue projection is not
with the spike
* The four blocks marked with an asterisk are the ones the shorter lean canvas drops. Two of this venture's live decisions sit in them, which is why they are restored here.
What makes the column worth having

Seven of nine cost days, not months

All but two are runnable this semester. The remaining pair need a real deployment and are marked as such rather than quietly scheduled alongside the others.

Two tests, either one fatal, neither needing code

Both are conversations

The first kills the neutrality argument and halves the addressable market. The fifth kills the market-type declaration. Conversations are the cheapest possible way to be wrong.

What the canvas concedes

The weak block is weak honestly, and one partner is a tension

The team holds capability rather than assets, and the only candidate compounding resource cannot be tested before deployment. Consuming someone else's semantic layer is the highest-leverage integration available and deepens dependence on artifacts the customer maintains for another product. Worth doing, worth naming.

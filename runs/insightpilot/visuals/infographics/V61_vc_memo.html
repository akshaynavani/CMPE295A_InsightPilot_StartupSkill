InsightPilot · Technical investor memo

The mechanism that makes these products safe is the mechanism that makes them decline the work

Every serious attempt has converged on one correctness mechanism: require a human to pre-encode the semantics before the question is asked. It works, and it has a structural cost nobody has priced, because an ad-hoc question is by construction the one nobody anticipated. The alternative is not a better guardrail. It is to accept that a system spanning unmodelled sources will sometimes be wrong, and to make being wrong cheap to detect. The unit of value is the investigation rather than the query, it ships with a hop-level trace, and the bet is that verification is cheaper than re-derivation.
The demolition, category by category
Warehouse-native assistants the bundled incumbents
strong
Governance, lineage and compute inherited from the platform, zero data movement. One of them re-reads its own generated SQL, authors smaller statements to verify specific aspects, and regenerates. Verification by decomposition, arrived at independently, which corroborates the bet here
limit
All are single-source and effectively single-hop, each bounded by a pre-authored artifact: a thirty-table cap, a token-capped hand-maintained model, existing measures only, single-number metrics
why stuck
Removing the bounds reintroduces the unreliability the bounds were bought to prevent. The constraint is the product
ceiling
On one benchmark family, top systems reach 96.7% on a curated setting and 65.6% once semantic indirection and real project structure appear. On production-scale schemas, 30 to 36%. And no commercial product publishes any accuracy figure at all
Search-first analytics the purest market test
repriced
A secondary cleared at a 73.67% markdown against the last primary round. The let-non-analysts-self-serve thesis did not pay off at the price it was funded at. Its agent is also capped at twenty-five queries per user per month, which prices occasional use rather than a core loop
consequence
The beachhead here is the analyst, not the business user. A deliberate renunciation of the category's largest revenue segment, made on that evidence
Agentic notebooks the most honest competitor
strong
Covers fetch, analyse, model and visualise, and already solves auditability structurally. Every hop is a visible, editable, re-runnable cell. The notebook is the lineage
limit
The analyst still directs. No plan before execution, no replanning on invalidation, no dispatch across specialists. The unit is a notebook, not an investigation pursued to a verified answer
the bar
Any lineage claim has to beat "we show you every cell", and "our trace is prettier" does not
Open source nearest neighbour, two years ahead
honest
Thirteen thousand stars and ten thousand cloud users, with a semantic engine and its own modelling language. The difference is scope of loop, not novelty of approach, which is quantitative, and the memo states it rather than letting a reader discover it
The two that actually win and neither is a product
leader
Doing nothing. 76% have decided without consulting data, 60 to 73% of enterprise data is never used for analytics, and about 25% of employees use the BI tools their employer bought. Free, instant, and rational against a one-to-four-week wait
hardest
The analyst's own SQL never fails on correctness, only on latency. She is the verification step, and that asymmetry is why this targets verification cost rather than raw speed

Two failure modes, both live here. Two died of the curation-to-value ratio. Two died of gravity.
Why this does not become a warehouse feature
The value is in spanning, and no warehouse vendor can sell spanning. A feature treating an operational database, a file and an API as equal peers to the lakehouse argues against its own gravity. One incumbent's design says so out loud: its reliability strategy is a curated space of at most thirty tables. Another is single-platform by construction.
Open core and self-hostable is what makes the claim credible rather than convenient. Neutrality asserted by a hosted service that sees all the customer's data is a claim. Neutrality backed by code the customer runs in their own environment, on their own key, is a property.
The honest residual. Neutrality is a structural answer, not an empirical one. If the first serious customer's questions all live in one warehouse anyway, the argument is true and worthless.
Three honest risks
Risk one · this ends it
Verification may not be cheaper than re-derivation

The beachhead's own words: verifying your reasoning is not obviously faster than writing it myself, I read SQL faster than I read explanations. That objection is correct as stated and unmeasured anywhere in the literature. If it holds, the plan gate and the verifier deliver an answer the analyst cannot use

mitigationTestable in one afternoon with five analysts and a stopwatch. It is the first experiment, and it precedes further building rather than following it
Risk two
The planner may not be reliable enough on real schemas

Roughly 15% on hard tasks against 76% on easy ones, over 450 tasks from a real financial-analytics workload. The field's weakest measured capability and this product's core dependency

mitigation, and its limitTyped plan structures, static validation, short plans with a hop ceiling, verification per hop. Mitigations, not solutions, and the planner is labelled research risk rather than presented as solved. The spike measures it and publishes the number, which is more than any competitor does
Risk three
The champion may capture the least value

Across one modelled day the staff scientist goes from two hours to nineteen minutes and the platform lead from a day and a half to eight minutes, while the analyst absorbs the review burden the other four shed. Since the whole go-to-market routes through her advocacy, a champion handed work while everyone else gets leverage does not advocate

mitigationTwo contingent things keep it viable: reviewing must be structurally cheaper than deriving, and the flywheel must reduce future review load. Review time per week is a first-class metric with queue depth as its leading indicator
What is not the top risk, and should not be presented as one: competition. The dominant risk is segment reality, and both halves of it are answerable in ten conversations with no code.
What would change this memo
→
Analysts accept answers they did not derive, faster than re-deriving. The thesis is validated
→
Fewer than a quarter of real requests span sources. Neutrality is true and worthless, the market halves, and the product should be rescoped
→
No buyer can name a budget line. This is a feature, not a company
→
The published accuracy number lands near the hard-task baseline. The mitigations do not work
Each is cheap to test and none has been tested. A memo claiming otherwise at this stage would be the graveyard's next entry.

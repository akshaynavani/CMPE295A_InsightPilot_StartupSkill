InsightPilot · Experiment board

Nine thresholds fixed in writing while they are still uncomfortable

A threshold set after seeing the data is a rationalisation, not a threshold. Every result below reads planned, which is the honest state. The column that makes the board real is the last one: what gets decided if the number comes in wrong.

E1
kills the company

Verification cost ratio

Five analysts, within subject, counterbalanced. Accept or reject an answer with lineage, against deriving the same answer from scratch. Correct answers only, so this measures cost rather than detection.

pass ≤ 0.5
amber 0.5 to 0.8
fail > 0.8

If it fails: stop and publish the negative result. A faster wrong-answer generator is not worth building, and the field has no public measurement either way.

result · planned
E2
the dangerous twin

Silent-error detection

Same analysts, later session. Six investigations, three carrying a planted defect: a wrong join grain, a wrong region mapping, a wrong time window. Control group sees answer plus SQL, the incumbent experience.

pass ≥ 70% and ≥ 2× control
fail < 50% or no lift

If it fails: the trace is comfortable rather than effective, which is worse than E1 failing. Redesign the verify surface before building anything further.

result · planned
E3
rescope trigger

Do real questions span sources

Ten analysts open their actual ticket history and classify their last twenty real requests by source count. Past behaviour, not estimation. Separate systems of record, not two tables in one warehouse.

pass ≥ 25%
amber 15 to 25%
fail < 15%

If it fails: the neutrality argument is true and worthless, addressable market halves, and the product narrows toward single-warehouse depth where it loses on distribution.

result · planned
E4
feature or company

Can a buyer name a budget line

Five budget owners, one question asked without pitching: what line would this come out of, and what would it displace. Answer recorded verbatim, then what they last bought against that line.

pass ≥ 3 of 5
fail ≤ 1 of 5, or "we would create one"

If it fails: the market-type declaration is wrong and the honest conclusion is a feature rather than a company. Acceptable for a capstone, decisive for a venture.

result · planned
E5
adoption stall

Analyst review load

Instrument review events from the first deployment: time from opening a review to sign-off, reviews per week, queue depth. Design-partner cohort kept separate so hand-holding does not contaminate the signal.

pass < 4 h/wk, flat
amber 4 to 8 h
fail > 8 h or rising

If it fails: the champion was handed work while everyone else got leverage. The fix is a design change, trust levels per question shape, not abandonment.

result · planned
E6
the engineering risk

Multi-hop execution accuracy

Harness first. Thirty to fifty questions over a known two-source schema with ground-truth results, scored strictly, with a per-hop-count breakdown so compounding is visible. Question set published.

pass ≥ 50%
amber 30 to 50%
fail < 30%

If it fails: narrow the spike to two-hop questions and report honestly. Publishing a low number is still the only public contribution available, since no commercial product publishes one.

result · planned
E7
largest soft number

Verifier coverage

Two defect classes. Countable ones, such as fan-out and coverage loss and null spikes. Plausible ones, where row counts are right and the meaning is wrong. Catch rate measured per class.

pass ≥ 60% overall and ≥ 30% plausible
fail < 30%, or near zero on plausible

If the plausible class fails: state that the verifier catches mechanical and not semantic failures. Narrower, still useful, and it makes the shown plan the primary defence instead.

result · planned
E8
the channel gate

Time to first investigation

Published repository with a container quickstart. Instrumented from clone timestamp through first connector, first approved plan, first signed-off answer. Self-serve split from design partners.

pass < 30 min
amber 30 to 60
fail > 60 min

If it fails: self-serve does not work and the real motion is design-partner led, which rewrites the channel plan. That was the only channel with viable economics at this contract value.

result · planned
E9
buyer objection

Semantic-layer lift

Same question set, same schema, two configurations. One with the dbt manifest available to the resolver and one without. Measured as a percentage-point difference in execution accuracy.

pass ≥ 15 pp
amber 5 to 15
fail < 5 pp

If it fails: consuming an existing semantic layer delivers less than claimed, which weakens the answer to the buyer's hardest objection and moves that principle to the roadmap's edge.

The most consequential process decision in the pack

Twenty-five hours that can invalidate the venture come before a semester of engineering

Waves one and two need no code at all. Running them first is not intuitive, because it means the opening month of a build project contains almost no building, and it is the ordering the whole plan rests on.

Why E1 and E2 are separate

Cheap checking and working checking are different claims

One measures the cost of checking a correct answer, the other whether checking catches a wrong one. Pass the first and fail the second and the product ships confident errors wearing an audit trail.

What the board deliberately omits

No traction rows, and no "validate demand"

There are no users, no revenue and no pipeline, so a board reporting any would be inventing them. Validating demand is not a hypothesis, and E3 and E4 are what it decomposes into once made falsifiable.

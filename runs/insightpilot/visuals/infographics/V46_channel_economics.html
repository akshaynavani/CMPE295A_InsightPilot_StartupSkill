Why self-serve works: the usual problem inverts. Marginal cost to acquire the next user is about zero and the fixed cost is engineering hours on documentation and the quickstart, amortised across every install forever. The constraint stops being cost per acquisition and becomes conversion quality, governed by one number: median time from clone to first successful investigation, with the threshold at thirty minutes.

A 32% haircut is survivable in isolation. What is not survivable is what the partner is paid for. A reseller earns its margin by carrying a complex sale into an account it already owns, and this product's whole premise is that the analyst installs it without a sale. Paying 28% for a sales motion the product is designed not to need is paying for the wrong thing.
The tension worth naming

The only channel whose economics work produces no revenue

That is not a flaw in the analysis, it is the shape of open-core distribution: adoption precedes revenue because the source petal has no budget line. For the capstone it resolves cleanly, since monetisation is out of scope. For any investor framing it has to be said rather than glossed.

What would reverse the verdict

One question, ten seconds, asked early

How many analysts sit on the central team. A fifty-analyst team gives a forty-five-thousand-dollar contract, which makes inside sales viable and this page wrong.

Conflicts recorded so they are not created later

Cohorts must be instrumented separately

The first ten users get hands-on help that later ones will not, which gives a false read on the quickstart metric unless the two cohorts are measured apart. There is no reseller conflict because there is no reseller, which is written down so that adding one later is recognised as a real change.

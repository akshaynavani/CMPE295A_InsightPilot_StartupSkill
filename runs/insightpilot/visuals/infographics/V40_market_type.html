InsightPilot · Market type

The category is old and the capability is not, which is what makes this ambiguous

Budget exists, buyers exist, and nobody needs the phrase explained. By every conventional test that is an existing market. But what is being sold is not what that market buys, and naming the type wrongly is the most expensive early mistake available in either direction.

Existing

Dominant risk: losing on distribution
Cycle: shortest
Rejected. The bundled incumbents sit inside the warehouse and the most widely deployed BI tool already. On that axis this cannot win and should not try.
declared

Re-segmented, by different attribute

Dominant risk: the segment is not real, or is too small
Cycle: medium, you teach the distinction rather than the category
Re-segmented along verifiability across sources, with the enterprise data analyst as the beachhead rather than the business user the category spent a decade failing to serve.

New

Dominant risk: adoption, not competition
Cycle: longest, you fund education
Tempting and wrong. The graveyard proves demand was repeatedly found. Claiming it would license skipping the teardown and inventing an adoption curve.

Clone

Dominant risk: execution and local dynamics
Cycle: short
No proven model is being transplanted.
Four reasons the re-segmentation holds
1
The low-end play has been tried and marked down. The purest expression of letting non-analysts self-serve cleared a secondary at a 73.67% markdown against its Series F price. A re-segmentation into the business-user tier is a move this market has already priced, which is why the beachhead is the analyst.
2
The attribute is one the market is actively funding. 59% of decision-makers at large organisations are directing incremental budget to semantic layers, 24.9% name accuracy as their top reservation, and 71% of practitioners name wrong output reaching stakeholders as a top concern. The money is moving toward verifiability.
3
Incumbents cannot follow without contradicting their own design. Each buys correctness by bounding scope: a table cap, a token-capped model, existing measures only, single-number metrics. Removing the bound reintroduces the unreliability they were bought to prevent.
4
The empty quadrant is empty for a stateable reason. Many-hop execution carrying correctness in the answer measures about 15% on hard real-world tasks. The segment exists and the capability to serve it is marginal, which is exactly what makes this a bet rather than a parity race.
What the declaration commits us to
Sales cycle
Medium. The first conversation goes on how is this not the bundled one, so every narrative artifact budgets for it.
Positioning
Against the attribute, never against a product. Comparative accuracy claims are forbidden because no measurement supports one.
Capital
Moderate. No category education and no distribution war. The only type honestly executable by a capstone, which is itself a reason for suspicion.
Dominant risk
Segment reality, not competition. The named risk is that a real customer's questions live inside one warehouse anyway, making neutrality true and worthless.
Pricing
Price the investigation. Both incumbent shapes already point that way, billing per message or capping queries per month.
Beachhead
The analyst, non-negotiable. The low edge stays a design constraint that earns the conversational interface, not the first customer.
What proves it
An analyst accepting an answer they did not derive, faster than re-deriving it, on a question spanning two sources. One observation validates segment, attribute and differentiator together.
What revokes it
Analysts wanting a faster single-warehouse answer and not valuing lineage. That collapses to an existing-market play, and would be a reason to change the product rather than the marketing.
The strongest case against, at full strength

Verification may be a complaint rather than a budget

The buyers who fear wrong output are describing a reservation about adopting the technology, not a willingness to buy a separate product. Look at what they did with the money instead: they funded semantic layers inside platforms they already own. Observed spending supports the incumbents' re-segmentation, not this one.

The answer, and its limit

Budgeted, yes. Budgeted outside the platform, unknown

That spend is evidence verifiability carries money, which is the load-bearing half and more than most re-segmentations can show. Desk research cannot close the rest, so it is recorded as an open risk rather than resolved.

Three things that must not be quietly reversed

Distinction, beachhead, and where validation spends first

Sell the distinction and make no head-to-head claims. The analyst is the first customer. Segment reality outranks every competitor, so the first validation budget goes there and not to accuracy.

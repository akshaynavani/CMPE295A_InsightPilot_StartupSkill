# Competitor teardown

**What this is** — A mechanism-level teardown of every alternative an enterprise data analyst can actually choose, including the two that win most often — writing the SQL themselves, and doing nothing — followed by the positioning read and the differentiator decision.
**Why it exists** — ASSUMPTIONS A6 deferred the competitive differentiator to this file, on the explicit condition that it be established from **failure mechanisms, not marketing gaps**. Without that discipline the differentiator becomes "they're clunky, we're conversational," which is the claim every entry in the graveyard also made. This file is also the pack's insurance against the reviewer who has used Databricks Genie: it must be obvious the team knows what the incumbents do well.
**How to read it** — §2's *Where it fails mechanistically* column is the load-bearing content; §3 is the positioning read; §4 is the decision. A skeptic should attack §4, which declines to claim a 10x and must therefore justify why what it does claim is defensible at all, and §2's "do nothing" row, which is the true market leader.
**Depends on / feeds** — Inherits the landscape ordering from [landscape.md](landscape.md), cites through [sources.md](sources.md), and is bounded by ASSUMPTIONS [A2](../ASSUMPTIONS.md) (no manufactured 10x) and [A6](../ASSUMPTIONS.md) (this file closes it). ## 1. What counts as a competitor

Not "companies in the BI market." A competitor is anything that consumes the budget or the hour that this product wants. That makes the set: the warehouse-native assistants the analyst's employer already pays for; the BI copilots bundled into tools already deployed; the agentic notebooks the analyst would actually enjoy; the open-source stack they could self-host on Friday; **the SQL they can already write**; and **nothing at all**.

The last two are listed last but win most often, and §2 treats them at full weight.

## 3. The positioning read

### 3.1 The two axes that matter

Every axis that first suggests itself — conversational vs. dashboard, AI-native vs. AI-added, cloud vs. self-hosted — sorts the market into groups that behave identically. Two axes actually separate behaviour, and both fall out of the failure column above.

**Axis 1 — hops of the core loop executed autonomously.** From zero (do nothing; the analyst) through one (Cortex Analyst, Copilot, Genie, Spotter, Wren AI: question → answer, single warehouse, single query) to many (a plan spanning sources, with replanning when a result invalidates it). Only Hex is near the many end, and only with a human driving each hop.

**Axis 2 — where correctness is established.** Three positions: **pre-encoded** (a human authored a semantic model offline — Cortex, Copilot, Pulse, Genie spaces, Wren AI's MDL); **post-hoc by the human** (the analyst reads the SQL, or the notebook, and decides — Hex, and the analyst writing their own query); or **carried with the answer** (the system emits a trace structured so that verification is cheap). The third position is essentially unoccupied. Genie's Inspect is the nearest move toward it, and it verifies *within* a single query rather than across a plan `[S32]`.

### 3.2 Where the white space sits

**Many hops, correctness carried with the answer.** Nothing occupies it. The reason is not oversight — it is that multi-step data reasoning is genuinely unsolved: the best agents score roughly 14.55–16% on DABstep's Hard split against 76.39% on Easy `[S4]`, and τ-bench shows a GPT-4o agent above 60% `pass^1` collapsing below 25% `pass^8` `[S5]`. The white space is real, and it is empty for a reason the pack must state plainly.

### 3.3 The two failure modes the incumbents cannot design away

These are the mechanisms, and they are what A6 required the differentiator to be built from.

1. **Pre-encoded correctness cannot answer an unanticipated question.** Every incumbent that gets correctness right does so by requiring an artifact authored before the question was asked — and that artifact is bounded: 30 tables per Genie Agent `[S32]`, ~1 MB of YAML `[S36]`, existing measures only `[S42]`, pre-defined metrics `[S48]`. An ad-hoc question is precisely the one nobody anticipated. **The better the incumbent's correctness mechanism, the narrower the set of questions it will attempt.** That is a design tension, not a bug, and it does not close with better models.
2. **Plausible-but-wrong is undetectable at the point of delivery.** "Failure looks like a plausible but incorrect answer" `[S2]`, with schema-level errors — wrong column, semantic misinterpretation — accounting for 81.2% of 4,602 analysed failures `[S2]`, and execution accuracy dropping up to 24 points under schema evolution `[S2]`. The market has priced this: 71% of practitioners name incorrect or hallucinated outputs reaching stakeholders as a top concern `[S16]`, and 24.9% name accuracy and hallucination risk as their top reservation about GenAI in analytics `[S17]`. Every incumbent's answer is "show the SQL," which transfers the verification cost back to the analyst — and verification of a query you did not write is not obviously cheaper than writing it.

## 4. The differentiator — closing ASSUMPTIONS A6

**A6 is now closed.** The differentiator, derived from §3.3 rather than from any marketing gap:

> **The investigation, not the query, is the unit — and it ships with a hop-level trace that makes verification cheaper than re-derivation.**

Two mechanisms produce it, and both trace to a named failure mode:

1. **Plan-and-replan across heterogeneous sources** answers failure mode 1. Because the plan is produced at question time from whatever sources are connected, an unanticipated question does not require a pre-authored artifact scoped to one warehouse. This is what no incumbent in row 3–9 does.
2. **Lineage structured for verification, emitted per handoff** answers failure mode 2. Not "here is the SQL" but: sources touched, query run, transform applied, method chosen — each separately inspectable and re-runnable, so the analyst checks the hop they doubt rather than re-reading the whole chain. This is the mechanism behind the brief's outcome metric, *time-to-verified-answer*.

**Four honest constraints on that claim.** Stating them here is what separates this from the graveyard's marketing.

- **It is not a 10x claim, and ASSUMPTIONS A2 stands unchanged.** This is a *positioning* differentiator: a defensible statement of what is different, not a claim of order-of-magnitude leverage. A2 remains the accepted deviation, logged once; downstream artifacts must not inflate this into a 10x.
- **Hex's notebook is the real bar for mechanism 2** `[S54]`, not Genie's SQL display. Any lineage claim must beat "we show you every cell," and "our trace is prettier" does not.
- **Mechanism 1 is the riskiest assumption restated.** BRIEF.md already names error compounding across fetch → analyze → model → visualize as the thing that invalidates everything. DABstep `[S4]` and τ-bench `[S5]` say the field has not solved it. The differentiator and the riskiest assumption are the same claim seen from two sides, which is the correct place for a capstone spike to aim.
- **The ML agent is deliberately not the differentiator.** It is the widest capability-to-product gap in the landscape ([landscape.md](landscape.md) §7.2) and, per A2, the founder declined to lead with it. Leading with it here would contradict a logged decision and invite "why not just AutoML," which this pack cannot yet answer.

## 5. What this teardown does not establish

- **No head-to-head accuracy comparison is possible.** No commercial agentic-BI product publishes an execution-accuracy figure on any public benchmark (sources.md, *Named gaps* 2). Every comparison above is mechanism-level. Downstream artifacts must not imply a measured advantage over Genie, Cortex or Copilot — there is no measurement to imply one from.
- **The gravity question is open.** Sisu raised ≈$128.7M and ended as a Snowflake division `[S56][S57]`; Narrative Science ended inside Tableau `[S58]`. Neither failed technically. "Why does this not end as a Databricks feature?" is unanswered by this file; cross-source neutrality is the only structural answer the research supports, and phase 2 must claim it explicitly or concede it.
- **No customer evidence exists.** Everything here is desk research. The differentiator in §4 is a hypothesis about what analysts will value, and it is exactly what the validation layer must test first.

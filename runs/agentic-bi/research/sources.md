# Sources

**What this is** — The numbered citation register for the entire pack. Every `[Sn]` tag in any artifact resolves here to a title, URL, access date, and a note on what the source is good for.
**Why it exists** — This pack's central credibility risk is that its problem statement is *researched, not lived* (BRIEF.md, *Founder edge*). Without a register, a reader cannot separate a vendor's content-marketing number from a leaderboard reading, and the evidence layer collapses to "trust me." Named here, each claim is checkable in one click — and the weak sources are labelled weak rather than laundered.
**How to read it** — Confidence column first. A skeptic should attack every row marked **Low**: those are the numbers that must not carry a load-bearing argument, and *Reliability notes* states exactly where they were and were not used.
**Depends on / feeds** — Produced by phase 1 alongside [landscape.md](landscape.md), [competitors.md](competitors.md), [capability_table.md](capability_table.md) and [survey.md](survey.md). Every downstream artifact in this pack cites through this file.

---

All sources accessed **2026-09-08**. Publication dates are given where the source shows one; `n.d.` means the page carries no date, which is itself a reason for a lower grade.

**Confidence grades.** **High** = primary: leaderboard, arXiv or peer-reviewed paper, vendor's own documentation or pricing page, named survey with disclosed sample and field dates. **Medium** = credible secondary reporting of a primary fact. **Low** = vendor content marketing, undated blog, or a figure whose underlying study is not identified. Low-confidence numbers appear in this pack only as range indicators, never as sole support for a claim.

## Reliability notes

Three cautions govern how this register may be used downstream.

1. **The low-confidence rows are quarantined.** `[S19]`, `[S20]`, `[S23]`, `[S24]`, `[S31]`, `[S49]`, `[S52]`, `[S53]` are vendor or aggregator content with no disclosed underlying study. They appear in this pack only where the surrounding text names them as weak. In particular, **`[S20]`'s "80% of a data team's time" supports nothing anywhere in this pack** — it is registered so a later reader who meets the figure elsewhere knows it was found and rejected.
2. **`[S15]` is a citation chain, not a primary source.** IBM restates figures attributed to Sigma Computing, Sisense and Forrester. Those underlying reports were not directly reachable, so the one-to-four-week turnaround figure is graded Medium and is always presented as *reported by IBM, attributed to Sigma* rather than as an IBM finding.
3. **Eight rows are registered but deliberately uncited in phase 1.** `[S25]`–`[S28]` are BI market-size estimates, staged for the phase 2 TAM/SAM/SOM exercise; no phase 1 artifact makes a market-size claim, because the research layer had no argument that needed one and `quality-bar.md` property 2 penalises decorative numbers. Note the spread when phase 2 uses them — $39.79B, $41.16B, $43.7B and $50.4B for the same 2026 market, with CAGRs from 8.67% to 14.98% `[S26][S27][S28]` — which is itself the reason to report a range and both sources rather than pick one. `[S21]`, `[S23]`, `[S24]` and `[S68]` are corroborating or low-confidence rows kept for downstream phases that may need them.
4. **`[S18]` is roughly five years old.** The Fivetran/Wakefield fieldwork closed in October 2021, before the generative-AI cycle this pack depends on. It establishes that the overhead problem predates LLMs, which is genuinely useful, and nothing more. It must never be cited as a current measurement.

## Named gaps — searched for, not found

Recording these matters as much as recording what was found. The absence is itself a finding, and a later phase should not spend budget re-searching them blind.

1. **No per-analyst ad-hoc queue measurement from a disclosed study.** Every figure located for requests-per-analyst-per-week came from consultancy or vendor marketing with no stated methodology (`[S19]`, `[S20]`). Request *turnaround time* is sourceable; *queue length* is not. This is the load-bearing gap for ASSUMPTIONS A8 — see [survey.md](survey.md) §6.
2. **No published accuracy benchmark for any commercial agentic-BI product.** Databricks, Snowflake, Microsoft, ThoughtSpot and Tableau all describe mechanism and none publishes an execution-accuracy figure against any public benchmark. Every accuracy number in this pack therefore comes from an academic leaderboard or third-party analysis, never from a vendor's own claim.
3. **No post-mortem written by a founder of a failed natural-language-BI company.** The graveyard below is assembled from acquisition records and product-retirement notices, `[S56]`–`[S60]`, which establish *that* attempts ended but rarely say *why* in the operators' own words. The nearest thing to a mechanism-level account is `[S2]`, written by a vendor still in the market and therefore not disinterested.
4. **No disclosed pricing for Zenlytic, AnswerRocket or Tellius.** All three are sales-qualified. `[S61]` gives Tellius's total raised as the only hard number obtainable for any of them.
5. **Google Cloud Data QnA's status could not be established.** It was searched for as a likely graveyard entry and no authoritative deprecation notice was found. It is therefore omitted from [landscape.md](landscape.md) rather than asserted.

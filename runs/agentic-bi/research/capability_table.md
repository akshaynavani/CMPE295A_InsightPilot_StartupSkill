## 1. Reading the table

All figures accessed 2026-09-08. Two cautions apply to every row.

**Benchmark scores are not deployment accuracy.** Annotation errors in the standard text-to-SQL benchmarks are documented well enough that leaderboard deltas of a few points carry no meaning `[S12]`. Treat every number as an ordering signal, not a specification.

**The spread across settings is the finding, not the headline.** The single most important reading in this file: on Spider 2.0, top systems reach **96.70% on the Snow setting** but fall to **65.60% on the DBT setting** `[S1]` — the same class of system, the same benchmark family, a 31-point drop when semantic-layer indirection and real project structure are introduced. Any argument built on the headline number is wrong in both directions.

## 3. What this means for "why now" — closing ASSUMPTIONS A9

A9 required both founder-asserted shifts to be dated and sourced, or dropped. Verdict on each:

**Shift 1 — "tool-use and replanning reliability crossed a usable threshold." Partially supported. Restate it.** The trend is real and dated: horizon doubling every ~7 months over six years and closer to every 4 months across 2024–2025 `[S6][S7]`. But the claim as written says a *threshold* was crossed, and the direct evidence contradicts that for this task class — roughly 14.55–16% on DABstep's Hard split `[S4]`, `pass^8` below 25% `[S5]`, and error propagation named as the primary reliability bottleneck `[S10]`. What crossed a threshold is **the ability to attempt the workflow**, not the reliability of its output. The honest form:

> Agentic tool-use became reliable enough that a chained fetch → analyze → model → visualize workflow is worth building and measuring — not reliable enough to trust unverified. That is precisely why lineage is load-bearing rather than cosmetic, and why the 295A spike tests one narrow path rather than the full loop.

This is a stronger position than the original, not a weaker one: it converts the brief's central risk into the justification for its signature feature. Downstream artifacts must use this wording and drop "crossed a usable threshold."

**Shift 2 — "tool access standardised (MCP, agent frameworks)." Fully supported and now dated.** MCP was announced 2024-11-25 with a Postgres reference server among the originals; OpenAI adopted it in March 2025, Google DeepMind and Microsoft mid-2025, AWS late 2025, and it was donated to the Linux Foundation's Agentic AI Foundation in December 2025 `[S62]`. That is a genuine standardisation event inside the brief's stated 18–24 month window, with four named adopters and a neutral steward. The one correction: it standardises *access*, not *semantics* `[S65]`, so it makes the connector layer tractable — the brief's actual claim — and does nothing for the schema-understanding problem that causes 81.2% of failures `[S2]`.

**A9 is closed** on those terms: shift 2 stands as written; shift 1 must be restated as above wherever it appears.

## 4. The three capabilities that must hold for the mechanism to work

Ordered by risk, highest first. This is the direct input to the validation layer's riskiest-assumption ranking.

1. **Multi-hop planning on real data (row 2).** Measured at ≈14.55–16% on hard real-world tasks `[S4]`. If this does not hold, the Orchestrator produces plans that fail silently and the product is a slower way to be wrong. **This is BRIEF.md's riskiest assumption, and the table confirms it is correctly identified.**
2. **Error compounding across four to six hops (row 4).** Structural and multiplicative `[S13]`; no model improvement removes it `[S10]`. Mitigations are architectural: per-hop verification, replanning on failure, short chains. Genie already ships the first of these inside a single query `[S32]`.
3. **Consistency across repeated attempts (row 3).** `pass^8` below 25% `[S5]`. An analyst who gets two different answers to one question has been given work, not leverage.

Rows 1, 6, 8, 9 and 10 are enabling and broadly favourable. Rows 2, 3 and 4 are the ones that decide whether the venture is real.

## Recommended next 3

1. **Make the 295A spike measure row 2 directly, on one narrow path, and publish the number.** No commercial agentic-BI product publishes an accuracy figure (sources.md, *Named gaps* 2). A capstone that measures its own multi-hop accuracy against a fixed question set produces something the entire incumbent field does not — and it tests the riskiest assumption instead of deferring it.
2. **Propagate the restated shift 1 into every artifact that carries a "why now."** The narrative layer and the whitepaper both will. The restatement is defensible under questioning; the original is not.
3. **Give the ML agent an explicit time budget in the PRD.** MLE-bench's headline is a 12-hour budget `[S9]`; the brief's outcome metric is wall-clock. Specify what the ML agent does in minutes — baseline model, feature suggestion, honest uncertainty — or the product layer will inherit a contradiction it cannot resolve.
